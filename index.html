01 — About

Comfortable on either side of the dashboard.

I got here by accident, a little bit. At 19 I started a food business out of pure curiosity about whether people would pay for what I was making — they did, and it taught me more about pricing, demand, and operations than any course could.

Since then I've worked the finance side (KPI dashboards, variance analysis, forecasting) and the growth side (attribution modeling, funnel analysis, campaign ROI) — often on the same team, sometimes in the same week. That range is on purpose: I'd rather stay useful across both than specialize too early into just one.

I graduated from Waterloo, I'm now based in New Delhi, and I'm actively figuring out what's next. Wherever that lands, the habit stays the same — find the signal in the noise, then actually do something about it.

02 — Off The Dashboard

The spreadsheets don't tell you this part.

Four things that are true about me, regardless of the job title.

Creative
Naturally, not by training
I don't wait for a brief to tell me what's possible. Whether it was building a food brand from nothing or finding a new angle in a campaign, I default to making something, not just analyzing what already exists.
Organized
Systems over stress
I'd rather build the dashboard once than re-explain the answer five times. I keep things structured enough that nothing depends on me remembering it — a habit that started with juggling a startup and a full course load at once.
Adaptive
Comfortable being new at things
Four jobs, four very different industries — automotive, hospitality, legal-tech, and my own startup. I've learned to get useful fast in unfamiliar rooms, and I actually enjoy the part where I don't know the answer yet.
People-first
I'd rather be in the room
The data's the excuse to have a better conversation, not a replacement for one. I like working cross-functionally, leading small teams, and being the person people actually want to loop in.
